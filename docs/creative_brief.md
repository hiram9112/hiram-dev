# Hiram Dev — Creative Brief

## Project

Hiram Dev is a personal technical profile and engineering casebook.

## Audience

Recruiters, engineering managers, CTOs, developers involved in hiring, agencies,
consultancies and technical partners evaluating whether they would be interested
in working with Hiram.

## Visitor job

Understand what Hiram can actually build, how he approaches engineering work,
what technical depth his work demonstrates, and whether he could be a good fit
for a team, collaboration or project.

## Mood

Deliberate. Distinctive. Technically grounded.

## Not

A résumé rendered as a website.

A conventional developer portfolio.

A technology showcase.

## The one memorable thing

Create one signature moment that could only belong to Hiram Dev.

It may use scroll, motion, interaction or another visual mechanism.
Choose the idea and execution yourself.

Everything else should support it rather than compete with it.

## Experience

The page should unfold progressively through scrolling rather than feel like
a stack of independent portfolio sections.

Scrolling, motion and interaction may be central design materials. They can be
used to create progression, reveal, transformation, spatial change or any other
mechanism that strengthens the experience.

The visual language, composition, typography, color and interaction model are open.

## Sources

Use `docs/product.md` as the source of truth for product purpose, audience,
positioning, scope and success criteria.

Use `docs/evidence.md` as the factual source for Hiram's demonstrated work.

Use `docs/engineering.md` as the technical source of truth for the current
stack, constraints and deferred implementation decisions.

Select and prioritize evidence rather than trying to reproduce everything.

## Motion

Scroll-linked motion is encouraged.

Motion may carry a significant part of the experience.

Choose the techniques required by the concept.

Respect reduced motion.

Design for phone as well as desktop.

## Do not use

Unsupported claims, invented experience or capabilities, or presentation that
suggests a level of seniority the evidence does not support.
